    How to Cut, Score & Assemble Clean Maquettes

    RED = FULL CUT
    BLUE = HALF SCORE
    TECHNIQUE A Normal Home/Office Printer

    1. Print on Paper → Mount on Carton

    Option 1: Load 250g–300g Bristol card directly into your normal paper printer.
    Option 2: Print on normal 80g copy paper, apply a thin layer of Glue Stick (not wet liquid glue!) to the back, and press it flat onto 1.5mm Greyboard/Carton.

    GOLDEN RULE #1 Crisp 90° Edges

    2. Score BLUE Lines First, Cut RED Second!

    Always run your cutter lightly along all Blue Dashed Lines FIRST while the sheet is still one large, stable piece held under your steel ruler. Only cut the outer Solid Red Lines at the very end!

    JOINERY Zero Glue Stains

    3. Toothpick PVA Glue & Slotted Joints

    Never squeeze glue directly from the bottle onto your maquette! Put a drop of white wood/PVA glue on scrap card and apply tiny dots to the grey glue tabs using a wooden toothpick. Hold corners square for 15 seconds.

    LASER / CNC Adobe Illustrator Ready

    4. Using the Downloaded SVG on a Laser Cutter

    Click "Download SVG / XML Vector" and open the file in Adobe Illustrator. The file is already pre-grouped into 3 standard laser layers: 01_CUT_RED, 02_SCORE_BLUE, and 03_LABELS_BLACK.

    PAGE 3 OF 3 // USING GEMINI OR ANY AI IMAGE WITH TAB 3

    Make the Image the Tool Can Read

    TAB 3: IMAGE → CUT SHEET
    WORKS OFFLINE, NO UPLOAD

    The tracer in Tab 3 measures a flat picture. It can only be as accurate as the image you give it. The ideal input looks like an architect's elevation drawing: one view, straight-on, no perspective, plain white background, no shadow, no floor, the whole object visible, in a solid dark colour. Below are two ready prompts — one to generate such an image, one to convert an image you already have.

    ✅ Tool-ready image checklist

    • Side or front elevation, camera at mid-height, looking straight at the piece (orthographic). Verticals must be vertical.
    • Plain, even background — pure white is best. Any single flat colour also works.
    • No cast shadow, no floor line, no reflection, no room.
    • Whole object inside the frame with a margin; nothing cropped.
    • One piece per image. Dark solid colour, no texture or pattern needed.
    • Legs, arms and back must be visibly separated from the background (a dark chair on white, never white on white).

    ❌ What makes the trace wrong

    • ¾ perspective / "product shot" angle — the far side is foreshortened; your scale will be wrong even after calibration.
    • Room scenes (rug, wall, window) — the background cannot be separated. Use Hand-trace or convert the image first.
    • Soft drop shadow glued to the legs — the tool removes most of it; a very dark shadow still needs the "Clean edges" slider or a point deleted by hand (Alt-click).
    • Transparent chairs, glass, chrome, white-on-white — no contrast, no outline.
    • Cushions, throws, plants on the piece — they become part of the silhouette.

    Prompt A — GENERATE a new tool-ready image (Gemini, ChatGPT, Copilot, Ideogram…)

    Create a technical 2D SIDE ELEVATION drawing of a [DINING CHAIR / LOUNGE SOFA / COFFEE TABLE / BED / WARDROBE] in a [Scandinavian / Mid-century / Minimalist] style.

Strict requirements:
- View: exact side elevation, orthographic projection, camera at mid-height, no perspective, no 3/4 angle, no foreshortening. All vertical edges perfectly vertical.
- Background: pure flat white (#FFFFFF). No gradient, no vignette, no room, no wall, no floor line.
- No shadow, no reflection, no ground contact shadow, no ambient occlusion.
- Whole object fully inside the frame with 10% white margin on every side, centred, nothing cropped.
- Render the object in one solid dark matte colour (charcoal #2B2B2B) with crisp clean edges. No texture, no wood grain, no fabric pattern, no highlights.
- Nothing else in the image: no text, no labels, no dimension lines, no watermark, no people, no cushions, plants or accessories.
- Realistic proportions: seat height 45 cm, total height 85 cm (adjust to the piece).
- Square image, 2048 × 2048 px.

Then produce a second image with the same object and the same rules as a FRONT ELEVATION.

    Edit the words in [brackets]. Ask for the side view for chairs, sofas and beds (it gives the profile you cut twice), and the front view for shelves, cabinets and tables.

    Prompt B — CONVERT an image you already have (upload it to Gemini, then paste this)

    Use the attached image as the reference. Keep the exact same piece of furniture — same design, same proportions, same number of legs, same back and arm shape — but redraw it as a clean technical 2D SIDE ELEVATION.

Strict requirements:
- Remove the entire background, room, floor, wall, rug and all other objects. Replace with pure flat white (#FFFFFF).
- Remove every shadow and reflection, including the ground-contact shadow under the legs.
- Rotate the view to an exact side elevation (orthographic, no perspective, camera at mid-height). Do not change the furniture's real proportions while doing this: a 45 cm seat stays at 45 cm relative to an 85 cm back.
- Draw the object as one solid dark matte charcoal silhouette (#2B2B2B) with crisp edges. No texture, no highlights, no colour, no outline strokes.
- Remove cushions, blankets, plants, books and any accessories that are not part of the furniture structure.
- Whole object fully visible and centred with a 10% white margin; nothing cropped.
- No text, labels, dimension lines or watermark. Square image, 2048 × 2048 px.

If the reference shows a 3/4 view, infer the true side elevation from it; do not keep the angled view.

    Check the result against the ✅ checklist before you upload it to Tab 3. If Gemini keeps the shadow, reply: "Remove the shadow under the legs completely; the background must be 100% white everywhere."

    15-Minute Studio Pilot Protocol

    What Is Tested vs. What to Pilot with 2 Students

    Verified by automated browser tests (Oct 2026):

    • Exact metric scale math (`(cm × 10) ÷ Scale`) for `1:10` to `1:50` and custom ratios.
    • True `210×297 mm` (A4) and `297×420 mm` (A3) SVG coordinate spaces.
    • Automated step-error detection (missing ID, seat height > total height, page overflow, and thickness conflicts).
    • Every part is drawn at true scale; if it doesn't fit the sheet the tool says so instead of shrinking it.

    3 Physical Checks to Run Before Full Class Launch (15 Mins):
    1. The Printer "Fit-to-Page" Trap (3 mins): Click Test 1 (1:20 Chair) and print 1 sheet on the actual print shop or studio printer. Measure the 5 cm Check Ruler in the top-right corner with a metal ruler. If it measures `4.7 cm`, the printer had "Fit to Printable Area" checked instead of "100% / Actual Size".
    2. Local Carton Caliper Check (5 mins): Measure the exact greyboard or maquette carton your students buy from the local stationery store. A board sold as `1.5 mm` or `2.0 mm` is often `1.7 mm` in reality—students can type that exact number in Custom Caliper (mm) so slots fit snugly.
    3. 2-Student "Blind" Trial (7 mins): Give the HTML file and a phone photo of a chair to 2 first-year students without helping them. Watch if they instinctively follow Steps 1 → 2 → 3 and score the Blue Dashed Lines before cutting the Red Perimeter Lines.
