Steps

    How to read it. White is the printed side of the card and brown is the back. The sheet rule says the printed side stays outside and every blue line folds away from you (a mountain fold). A fold that goes the other way is drawn in orange and listed as a valley fold. Waffle ribs that need glue are orange.

    What the order is. For a net, the order is the one the checker used: it starts from the base panel and works outward, panels first, then glue tabs, then the separate parts. That order is valid, but it is not always the easiest order for a student's hands. For waffle, the order is the assembly plan printed on the sheet.

    Side profiles. Each side is placed with its printed side facing out, as the sheet rule says, and the page then reports where its post lands. In v4.3.0 and v4.2.4, B2 RIGHT was drawn identical to B1 LEFT, so with its printed side out its post sits at the front (fault F2, fixed in v4.3.1 by drawing B2 mirrored). Curved backs. From v4.3.2 the curve offsets are real centimetres, so an arched back is the same design at 1:10 and 1:20; in v4.3.1 the same preset rose twice as high at 1:20 (fault F1). These pages use 1:20.

    Limits. The card has zero thickness. Overlap is checked only in the finished shape, so two panels passing through each other during a fold would not be caught. Separate parts are placed by a stated rule rather than searched for. Physical fit, glue strength and paper stretch are tested in the studio (gates B2–B11).
